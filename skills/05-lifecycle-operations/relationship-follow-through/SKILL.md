## The judgment, in one screen

**What this skill prevents:** two failure modes that quietly kill networking value — messaging out of anxiety (burning a real person's goodwill on a touch that serves the sender's nerves, not the relationship) and dropping real commitments (a scheduled call, an owed reply, a promise someone actually made) because they lived in memory instead of a ledger.

**The calls it hardcodes** — each written down because improvising it went wrong:

- **Facts come from the captured record, never from memory.** Judgment runs on the complete stored conversation — every message, direction, and timestamp — not on previews, screenshots, or recollection. If the record is incomplete, the output is a repair instruction, not a draft: advice built on a partial thread is confident guessing about a real person.
- **The state decides the action.** Every relationship lands in exactly one attention state, and the state — not the operator's mood — determines what happens next. Waiting and closing are *correct outcomes*, not failures to act.
- **One open loop per message.** Before a word is drafted: *why reply now, and which decision improves once they answer?* A message that advances two things advances neither.
- **Ask what only they can answer.** Questions target the person's lived judgment — what matters most in this work, what gets underestimated — never "any advice?", never facts a search would answer, never a homework packet disguised as curiosity.
- **A title is not authority.** A manager title alone is not hiring-manager evidence, and a recruiter is a routing owner — how the application moves, never what the role truly is.
- **Attention must be re-earned.** The first message after an acceptance needs no new excuse — it opens the relationship. Every follow-up after that must justify itself with something person-specific, and silence gets a floor of 7–10 working days before another touch.
- **Advocacy is asked for late.** A referral request waits until the person could honestly retell the candidate's fit in two or three sentences — with one exception: when they volunteer that a window is closing, one clear ask with an easy out is allowed.

**One call, worked** (from [the controlled-run receipt](../../../examples/one-brief-controlled-run.md)): a sync round ends with one confirmed interview-scheduling thread and five relationships owed a reply. The readout ranks the scheduling confirmation first — a commitment made to a real person outranks a follow-up queue — and that ordering *is* the judgment: follow-through triage is about who is actually waiting on you, not who you feel behind on.

## The attention states

| State | What it means | The action |
|---|---|---|
| **Scheduled next step** | They committed to a call, an intro, a concrete action | Confirm logistics, prepare for the interaction — this outranks everything |
| **Reply now** | A substantive inbound sits unanswered | Answer what they actually said before advancing anything of yours |
| **Open relationship** | Accepted, conversation not yet started, opportunity still live | Open warmly within two working days — a low-effort, person-specific start |
| **Wait** | The last word was yours, or nothing person-specific has changed | No draft. Waiting is the move |
| **Close or repair** | Wrong person, closed pursuit, natural ending, or a data gap | Close cleanly or fix the record — this never occupies people-time |

Triage across many threads follows the table's order. The daily attention budget goes to the top three states; the bottom two protect it.

## The drafting contract

A draft exists only for threads worth advancing, and it follows a human line of thought, not a template: *acknowledge what they actually said → offer a tentative understanding that leaves room to be corrected → ask the one question that improves a real decision.* AI appears in a message only where it is genuinely part of the candidate's work and relevant to the exchange — one bounded, concrete sentence, never a pitch, never an assumption about the other side's stack.

Each deliverable pairs the full stored conversation with the ready-to-send draft, so the human reviews the message in its complete context — and the human sends it. Commitments and outcomes (a useful conversation, a promised intro, a scheduled step, a dropped commitment) are recorded in the relationship ledger only after a human-confirmed read of the full thread, never auto-inferred from message text.

## What this skill never does

- Never sends anything — every message leaves by a human hand.
- Never drafts for a backlog: no thread earns a message just by existing.
- Never builds judgment on a partial or stale record; it repairs the record first.
- Never treats an acceptance as an owed reply, or a reaction as a response.
- Never invents claims about the other person's team, tools, or intentions to make a message land.
